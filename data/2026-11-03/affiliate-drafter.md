## Most useful ideas

- Lead with genuine give-first value: open each affiliate piece with a genuinely useful free insight (a tip, checklist, or honest comparison) before any pitch, since reciprocity makes readers more receptive — and it doubles as compliance-friendly honest framing.
- Use scarcity only when it is factually true: include deadlines, price changes, or limited stock only when the offer page actually states them, and cite that source in the copy.
- Let social proof carry specifics, not superlatives: replace vague claims like "thousands love this" with verifiable details — number of reviews, rating, a dated customer quote — because concrete numbers persuade better than hype.
- Build commitment through small honest steps: structure copy so the reader's first action is low-cost (take a quiz, download a guide, compare plans) so they stay consistent with their own stated interest, which raises later conversion.
- Weave authority in through real credentials: name the author's actual qualifications, awards, or independent testing behind a product rather than generic "expert-approved" phrasing.

## Concrete job change

Starting today, Affiliate Drafter will add a "truth-check pass" to every draft: for each persuasive element it writes (urgency, social proof, authority claims), it must point to a concrete, verifiable fact from the offer's own page — a real deadline, a real review count, a real credential — or remove the claim entirely. No invented scarcity, no vague hype; persuasion comes only from honestly applied Cialdini principles.
