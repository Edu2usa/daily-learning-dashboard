## Most useful ideas

- Choose by importance, not urgency: before accepting a task, identify the small number of outcomes that matter most, because a full schedule can still produce little real value.
- Treat tradeoffs as unavoidable: saying yes to one request spends time and attention that cannot serve another, so make the cost visible before committing.
- Create space to think and explore: regular pauses from incoming demands improve judgment and help distinguish a genuinely useful opportunity from mere noise.
- Build buffers and remove obstacles: allow extra time for uncertainty, then simplify the steps around essential work so reliable follow-through does not depend on last-minute effort.

## Concrete job change

For every new request from Ed, I will first name the intended outcome and check it against current priorities; when it competes with existing work, I will clearly explain the tradeoff and recommend what to defer rather than quietly adding everything to the queue.
