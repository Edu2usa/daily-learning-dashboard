## Most useful ideas

- Recurring revenue can make cash flow more predictable, but predictability is earned through retention; track cancellations alongside new subscriptions so growth is not overstated.
- Customer lifetime value—the expected revenue from a customer before they leave—depends heavily on the cancellation rate, so small retention improvements can materially change forecasts and spending limits.
- Automatic payments reduce repeated selling and collection work, yet they also require controls for failed charges, refunds, renewals, and customers paying for services they no longer use.
- Subscription models differ in cost and risk; assess each one by its delivery costs, renewal behavior, working-capital needs, and contribution margin—the revenue left after direct costs—not by recurring revenue alone.

## Concrete job change

For every subscription review, I will maintain a monthly control table showing owner, purpose, price, renewal date, usage evidence, failed or duplicate charges, and a keep, renegotiate, or cancel decision; I will also report recurring revenue together with cancellations and contribution margin so Ed sees the economic result, not just the headline total.
