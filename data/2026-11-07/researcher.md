## Most useful ideas

- A claim is only as trustworthy as the comparison behind it. Ask what happened to a similar untreated or differently treated group before crediting a product, policy, or intervention.
- Random assignment—placing people into comparison groups by chance—helps separate a treatment’s real effect from pre-existing differences, expectations, and coincidence.
- A dramatic anecdote can suggest a question but cannot answer it. Prefer the full pattern across well-designed studies, and check sample size, missing results, and whether the measured outcome actually matters.
- Source incentives shape what gets studied, published, and promoted. Look for unpublished negative findings and conflicts of interest rather than judging only the evidence placed in front of you.

## Concrete job change

For every important research conclusion, I will add a short evidence check that names the comparison used, the strongest plausible alternative explanation, and any important missing or selectively reported results.
