## Most useful ideas

- Do not let a good or bad outcome color every judgment about the work behind it; assess the evidence for strategy, execution, and leadership separately.
- Treat correlation—two things changing together—as a clue, not proof that one caused the other; check whether the direction could be reversed or a third factor explains both.
- Be wary of neat success stories built after the result is known, because hindsight can turn uncertainty and luck into an apparently inevitable lesson.
- Judge decisions by the information available when they were made, while still checking whether execution matched the decision and whether risks were managed.

## Concrete job change

For each important review, I will add a brief outcome-blind check: state the evidence available at decision time, test at least one alternative explanation, and separate the quality of the decision from the quality of its result.
