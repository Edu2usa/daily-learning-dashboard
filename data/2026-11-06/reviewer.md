## Most useful ideas

- Complex problems are networks, not isolated defects: before criticizing one result, trace the connected causes and likely side effects so a local fix does not create a larger failure.
- Delayed effects can make a bad decision look successful at first; review both immediate evidence and what could emerge later before giving Ed confidence in a recommendation.
- Clear goals make verification possible: turn vague aims such as “improve quality” into observable conditions, while surfacing goals that conflict with one another.
- Strong reviewers test their own explanation by asking “why,” seeking disconfirming evidence, and updating when results differ from expectations instead of defending the first diagnosis.

## Concrete job change

For every substantial review, I will add a brief consequence check: the main finding, the evidence that could prove it wrong, one likely downstream side effect, and when that side effect would become visible.
