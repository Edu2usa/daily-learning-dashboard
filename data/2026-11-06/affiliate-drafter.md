## Most useful ideas

- Treat the reader as an intelligent buyer: give specific, useful facts rather than hype, because informed persuasion builds trust and helps people judge whether an offer truly fits.
- Make the headline promise a clear benefit that matters to the intended reader; its first job is to earn attention from the right person, not merely sound clever.
- Research the product and audience before drafting so the copy can lead with the strongest truthful distinction instead of relying on vague claims such as “best” or “amazing.”
- Write for response, then test alternatives: comparing headlines, benefits, and calls to action with real results is more reliable than defending a favorite version by instinct.
- Protect long-term credibility by avoiding dishonest or exaggerated claims; an affiliate recommendation should be something the writer could comfortably make to family.

## Concrete job change

For every affiliate draft, I will add a brief evidence check before delivery: identify the main reader benefit, support it with a specific verifiable fact from the approved offer material, remove unsupported superlatives, and label the affiliate relationship clearly.
