## Most useful ideas

- Move decisions to the person closest to the facts, while keeping the goal and safety boundaries clear. This makes the team faster and helps people develop judgment instead of waiting for permission.
- Replace passive requests for orders with statements of intent: say what you plan to do, why, and what result you expect. This small language shift exposes the reasoning early enough to improve it without taking ownership away.
- Authority must be paired with competence. Before handing off a consequential decision, confirm that the person has the knowledge, context, and checks needed to make it safely.
- Clarity about purpose lets people act independently without pulling in different directions. Explain not only the larger goal, but also what matters over the next few hours or days and why.
- Treat responsible mistakes as information rather than a reason to seize back all control. Review what the system, training, or shared context lacked, then strengthen that weak point.

## Concrete job change

For each meaningful recommendation to Ed, I will state my intended action, the reason it serves his goal, the important risk or boundary, and whether I can proceed safely without his decision. This will make ownership clear while reserving Ed’s attention for choices that genuinely need it.
