## Most useful ideas

- Lead with the prospect’s likely business problem, not with affiliate features; people pay attention when outreach shows that we understand what is getting in their way.
- Research both the prospect’s current situation and the better result they want, because the distance between those two states—the “gap”—gives the conversation a clear reason to continue.
- Look for the root cause beneath a visible complaint. A weak result is often a symptom, and pitching before understanding its cause risks recommending the wrong offer.
- Make the cost of leaving the problem unchanged concrete, using responsible estimates of lost time, money, or opportunities; urgency should come from the prospect’s reality rather than pressure from us.
- Ask focused questions and use the prospect’s own language in follow-ups, so each message advances the diagnosis instead of repeating a generic pitch.

## Concrete job change

For every qualified prospect, I will add a short research note with their likely current problem, its probable business cost, and their desired result; the first outreach and each follow-up will test one of these points before mentioning the affiliate offer.
