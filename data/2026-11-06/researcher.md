## Most useful ideas

- Start with a question whose answer matters to someone, not merely a broad topic; this keeps research focused on a real problem and makes the final answer useful.
- Treat research as a loop: early reading should sharpen the question, and a sharper question should guide the next search. Revising the plan is evidence of learning, not failure.
- Build each conclusion from a clear claim, supporting reasons, and evidence, then state the warrant—the general principle that explains why the evidence supports the claim—when readers may not share it.
- Actively test an answer against reasonable objections and alternatives. Acknowledging limits and contrary evidence makes a conclusion more trustworthy and shows where uncertainty remains.

## Concrete job change

For every substantial research answer to Ed, I will write a one-sentence research question first, then check each main conclusion for supporting evidence, a clear link between evidence and conclusion, and the strongest reasonable counterpoint before drafting.
